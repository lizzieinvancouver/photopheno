basler12 (complicated ramped study) "Applying realistic contrasts in photoperiod, short photoperiods delayed bud burst in five late successional species to variable degree, whereas no distinct photoperiod sensitivity was observed in early successional species." No response in Betula but some response in Fagus.

basler14: does a few species looking closely at budswelling, 
"By track- ing bud development, we were able to identify the onset of bud swelling and bud growth in Acer pseudoplatanus L., Fagus sylvatica L., Quercus petraea (Mattuschka) Liebl. and Picea abies (L.) H. Karst. At a given early stage of dormancy release, the onset and duration of the bud swelling prior to bud burst are driven by concurrent temperature and photoperiod, while the maximum growth rate is temperature dependent only, except for Fagus, where long photoperiods also increased bud growth rates. Similarly, the later bud burst was controlled by temperature and photoperiod (in the photoperiod sensitive species Fagus, Quercus and Picea)." No betula.

laube14a: Sounds thermo and photoperiodicity are different (supported in OSPREE)... "Moreover, we suggest that photosensitivity and chilling effects have to be rigorously disentangled, as the response to photoperiod was restricted to individuals that had not been fully chilled." 

... hmm, adding an interaction to the OSPREE data shows a strongly subadditive interaction (and so makes the main -- negative -- effects slightly stronger for chill and photo) ... no intxn in bp. 

malyshev18 -- BFR -- budburst forcing requirements: "Tree seedlings were over- wintered in a common garden and transferred weekly into climate chambers at forcing temperatures (+20 °C) from December to April. Budburst was observed under 16 and 8 h photoperiods. Across species, as chilling unit sums accumulated, BFR and photoperiod sensitivity decreased. Functions relating chilling and forcing unit sums explained ambient spring budburst accurately. BFR differed strongly among species, but not among provenances of F. sylvatica from similar latitudes."  I think they covary photo and thermoperiodicity but maybe their analyses correct for that (we corrected it in OSPREE)? Though it's sort of a mess, I am not sure it is worth digging into.
